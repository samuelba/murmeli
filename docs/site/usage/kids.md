# For kids

This page is for parents. It explains what your kid sees, so you can show them around the first time. Most screens have a speaker button that reads the text aloud, and for kindergarten kids the prompts are read aloud automatically (see the **Read aloud** setting in [For parents](parents.md#create-a-kid-profile)).

The buttons in the learning area are big, so small fingers can hit them on a tablet. Everything also works with a keyboard.

## Pick your profile
The start screen asks **Who's learning today?** Tap your own animal.

If your profile has a lock, Murmeli asks for your 4 pictures. Tap them in the right order. The delete button removes the last one. If you get it wrong too often, Murmeli says it is time for a short break, and you can try again a bit later.

The **Parents** button is for grown-ups.

## The trail map
Every subject is a mountain trail. Your kid climbs from the valley at the bottom up to the summit.

- **Tabs** at the top switch between the subjects, for example math and reading.
- **Worlds** are the stretches of the trail, like the Zahlenwiese (number meadow) or the Lautwiese (sound meadow). Each world has its own landscape.
- **Levels** are the yellow hiking signposts. Each shows up to 3 stars.
- **Murmeli the marmot** stands at the next level to play ("You are here").
- A **locked** level shakes when tapped and says to play the others first. A level opens when the one before it has at least 2 stars. A new world opens when the worlds before it are done.
- A finished world gets a **summit flag**.
- **Practice** (the yellow button) mixes exercises from levels your kid has already opened.
- **Switch profile** goes back to the profile picker.

## Playing a level
A level is a short round, usually 6 exercises. The dots at the top show how many are left.

1. **Show me:** the first time your kid opens a level, Murmeli shows how the exercise works, step by step, and says what to do. **Show again** repeats it, **Let's go!** starts the round. In every later round, the **Show me** button brings the demo back.
2. **Answer:** tap the answer, or for some exercises (number pad, number house, number wall, number line, word building) put it together and tap **Check**.
3. **Feedback:** a right answer gets a cheer. After the first wrong answer, a **Tip** explains how to think about it, and your kid tries the same exercise again. After a second wrong answer, Murmeli marks the right answer and the round goes on with **Next**.
4. **Stop:** the stop button at the top asks **Do you want to stop?** **Keep playing** goes back, **Yes, back to the map** ends the round. A round that was stopped doesn't count against your kid.

### Special exercises
- **Tracing letters:** trace the letter with a finger, a stylus or the mouse, starting at the numbered dot and following the arrow. **Again** clears the drawing, **Done** checks it, and **Show me how** lets Murmeli draw it first. Without a pointer, the arrow keys move a pen, the space bar puts it down and lifts it, and Enter checks.
- **Paying:** tap coins and notes in the wallet to put them on the tray; tap a coin on the tray to take it back, then **Pay**. Any combination with the right amount counts.
- **Clock:** read the time on the clock and pick it. German uses Swiss phrasing: "halb drei" is 2:30.
- **Listening and writing:** listen to the word (the big button repeats it) and put the letters in order.

## Stars
At the end of a round, your kid gets 0 to 3 stars:

| Stars | When |
|---|---|
| 3 | every exercise right on the first try |
| 2 | at least 80% (most levels): the level is passed and the next one opens |
| 1 | at least half |
| 0 | less than half |

A right answer on the second try counts half. Only the best result of a level is kept, so playing again can only add stars. From the results screen, **Next level**, **Play again** or **Back to the map**. When a new world opens, the results screen says so.

There are no streaks and nothing is ever taken away.

## Practice
**Practice** builds a mixed round of 8 to 10 exercises from up to 3 skills: first the ones that are due for repetition, then ones not played yet, then the weakest. It never changes the stars on the map. Skills that went well come back after longer and longer breaks (1, 2, 4, 8, then 16 days), so practice stays short and useful.

## Badges
The trophy button on the trail map opens the badge shelf. Earned badges shine; the others say how to get them: the first passed level, the first completed world, 5 levels with 3 stars, and the first practice round. New badges are celebrated at the end of a round.

## Breaks
After about 20 minutes of playing, Murmeli suggests a short break between two exercises. **Keep playing** goes on, **Take a break** goes back to the profile picker.

When the daily learning time set by the parent is used up, starting a new round shows a sleeping Murmeli: "That's enough learning for today. See you tomorrow!" A round that is already running can be finished first.
